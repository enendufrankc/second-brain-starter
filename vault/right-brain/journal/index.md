### 2026-04-25 — The Wake-Up Call

Today was a turning point. Two back-to-back financial sessions — the first grill set up the 5-phase plan to £1m, and then the bank statement deep dive blew up every assumption I had about my spending.

Key realisations:
- I was spending £1,280/month on variable costs, not the £500 I told myself
- Klarna had me hooked — £715 in January alone, averaging £458/month on shopping
- Nnenna's transfers were completely unbudgeted — £1,604 in March
- I had a Moneybox LISA running at £334/month while carrying 44.9% APR credit card debt
- Clinton got £890 in loans I shouldn't have given

What I did about it:
- Rewrote the entire financial plan with real numbers
- Created a 6-month stabilisation sprint (May–Oct 2026)
- Set 11 non-negotiable financial rules
- Put Nnenna on a fixed £600/month allowance
- Committed to deleting Klarna, pausing the LISA, and killing all consumer debt by July

This is the hardest but most honest financial work I've ever done. The numbers don't lie. Time to execute.
