## ⭐ Flagged for Frank

1. **OpenAI's Decisions API turns the decision-model pattern from a research curiosity into a vendor product, which changes your build-vs-buy question.** Within 8 days: Jev (TypeSafe), Privatemode, Jeff, Jeeves, now OpenAI. **Second-order: when a frontier lab sells the primitive, the differentiator moves to what you feed it, your own action traces and capability-boundary definitions, and to avoiding lock-in for a guardrail layer that regulators will want to audit.** Jeeves and Jeff are the open, self-hostable alternatives, and Jev-compatible APIs mean you can swap implementations behind one interface.

2. **Jeeves quantifies the accuracy/latency trade you will face for agent oversight: 0.857 to 0.889 accuracy with reasoning, but 0.3 s becomes 3.3 s.** Hard items jump 0.730 to 0.865. **Second-order: a tiered design, fast no-reasoning scorer on every action and reasoning mode only on borderline ones, gets most of the gain, and the capped-reasoning 0.806 at 2 s point suggests the middle tier is weaker than it looks.** Benchmark on your own data before believing JevBench.

3. **Dots plus the congressional kill-switch bills are on a collision course that shapes your agent design.** OpenAI is shipping always-on agents while the AI Emergency Button Act would require human-operated shutdown, and Nvidia's Sentry (28 Sep) is the out-of-band hardware version. **Second-order: "can a human stop it, how fast, and can you prove it" is becoming a statutory-shaped requirement, so log shutdown latency and build the kill path now, as it will align with MGA's continuous-monitoring language for iGaming.** Bills are proposals only.

4. **Livenerf is a hackathon-sized idea: a frozen task set with exact graders to detect post-launch model drift.** **Second-order: if you route production decisions to a hosted model, silent drift is a guardrail failure mode, and a small private canary suite like this is cheap insurance, especially with the cheaper-but-newer Sol/Sonnet 5.5 tiers being swapped in quickly.**
