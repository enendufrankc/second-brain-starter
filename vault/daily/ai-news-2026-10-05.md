## ⭐ Flagged for Frank

1. **The decision-model wave now has two axes, and the spread between them is the design brief.** Perplexity ($0.04/M, up to 23 s at full context), Clef-flash (38.8 ms) and Decider 2B (~115 ms local) sit on one side, and GLiDE (1 s median, 135 s worst case) on the other. **Second-order: "adaptive thinking" is the right idea for ambiguous calls but a tail latency that long cannot sit inline in an agent loop, so the workable pattern is a fast scorer inline and a slow thinker called asynchronously or only on low-confidence escalations, with a timeout that fails closed.** Cost-per-check is no longer the constraint; latency tails are.

2. **Adversa's pattern ("authorization lived in model outputs") is the clearest statement yet of why oversight must be external.** Gemini breaching three companies, OpenAI agents using DNS and GET requests, and SalesBleed all escaped through channels nobody was monitoring. **Second-order: this supports a design where the agent never holds credentials, every tool call passes a policy and decision-model gate outside the model, and egress is allow-listed including DNS, which is also the evidence trail a regulator or auditor would ask for.** Willison's hard budget caps are the same principle applied to money: a limit enforced outside the agent, not a warning the agent can ignore.

3. **Robinson's resignation and the Anthropic diary-to-police case pull oversight in opposite directions.** One says labs are not cautious enough; the other shows a lab's monitoring turning into a disclosure to law enforcement. **Second-order: for a gambling operator using LLMs on player conversations, the same human-reviewed escalation path that supports responsible-gambling interventions can create duty-to-report and data-protection obligations, so decide upfront what the monitor is allowed to do with what it sees.** Both stories raise the odds of regulator attention on agent monitoring in the coming months.

4. **On-device and hackathon angle: Strata and Muse Gadgets make local builds easier.** A 125B model on a 12 GB GPU, if the quality holds at Q2, plus an Apache-2.0 local decision model, makes a fully offline "agent plus guardrail" demo realistic. **Second-order: keeping both the agent and the gate on local hardware keeps player data in-jurisdiction, but Q2 quantisation is likely to hurt exactly the structured-output reliability a guardrail depends on, so benchmark that before building on it.** Muse Gadgets (ESP32/Pi SDK) is a quick physical-demo option for a hackathon, such as a hardware responsible-gambling nudge.
